COWS DIAMOND DA40 HOTKEY REFERENCE

Covers the DA40-NG and the DA40-XLS. Where the two differ, the difference is
noted in the aircraft-specific section at the end.

The DA40 is a single-engine GA aeroplane with a Garmin G1000 and a GFC 700
autopilot. It has no autothrottle, no Airbus FCU and no MCDU, so the FCU and MCDU
sections of the airliner guides do not apply here. Everything else below is the
standard MSFSBA set and works on this aircraft.

Application Navigation:
  Ctrl+1     Focus Sections List
  Ctrl+2     Focus Panels List
  Ctrl+3     Focus Status Display field of the current panel (refreshes it if empty)
  F5         Refresh the current panel's Status Display without leaving your place in it
  Ctrl+M     Monitor Manager - tick and un-tick which background changes are spoken

The Status Display is the primary readout on this aircraft. Every panel has one,
holding what the matching glance at the real panel would take in: the whole
electrical picture, the whole engine picture, and so on. Gauges with published
arcs report their arc with the value, so oil temperature reads "87 celsius,
green" rather than a bare number - the arc is what a sighted pilot actually sees.

Tip: Press the same bracket key again to cancel a mode you activated by accident.

OUTPUT MODE - Press right bracket to activate

Altitude:
  Q          Read Altitude AGL
  A          Read Altitude MSL

Airspeed:
  S          Read IAS (Indicated Airspeed)
  T          Read TAS (True Airspeed)
  G          Read Ground Speed
  M          Read Mach Number

Heading:
  H          Read Magnetic Heading
  U          Read True Heading

Navigation:
  N          Read NAV 1 and NAV 2 radio info (frequency, ident, localizer, glideslope, DME)
  Ctrl+W     Read ND active (TO) waypoint: name, distance, bearing

Fuel:
  F          Fuel in the TANKS - total and each tank. Answers in the units set on
             the MFD's Aux System Setup page; while that is the default gallons
             the AFM's litres figure is given beside it, as the AFM does.
  Shift+F    How long that fuel LASTS - current flow, endurance at that flow, and
             the difference between the tanks against the 9 gallon AFM limit.
             On the NG the tanks drift apart on their own: fuel is burnt from the
             main and returned through the aux.

             These two do NOT match the MFD's Fuel Calculator, and are not meant
             to. The calculator is a TOTALIZER - a figure you set yourself with
             the INC FUEL / DEC FUEL / RST FUEL softkeys on the Engine page, which
             then counts down by fuel USED. It is a flight-planning figure; these
             keys read the tanks.

Weight:
  W          Read Gross Weight (pounds) + center of gravity
  Shift+W    Read Gross Weight (kilograms) + center of gravity

Flaps:
  L          Read Flap Position

Landing Gear:
  Shift+G    Read Gear Position

Altimeter:
  B          Read Altimeter Setting (hPa and inHg, or Standard)

Vertical:
  V          Read Vertical Speed
  Ctrl+Shift+R  Read last landing rate (touchdown vertical speed, feet per minute)
  Ctrl+Shift+G  Read last landing peak g-force

Approach:
  Ctrl+0     Read Approach Capability (CAT rating)

Weather:
  I          Wind Info
  O          Read Outside Temperature (Celsius)

Time:
  Z          Read Local Time at aircraft position. Spoken as
             "HH:MM <time-zone name>" (e.g. "16:38 Eastern Daylight Time"
             near KJFK), or "HH:MM:SS <time-zone name>" (e.g.
             "20:30:45 British Summer Time") if "Include seconds in
             time announcements" is ticked in Announcement Settings.
             The time-zone name is looked up from the aircraft's
             geographic position and is DST-aware.
  Shift+Z    Read Zulu / UTC Time. Spoken as "HH:MMZ" (e.g. "03:30Z"),
             or "HH:MM:SSZ" (e.g. "00:15:30Z") when seconds are on.

Transponder:
  X          Read Squawk Code

Distance:
  D          Read Distance to Destination (from the FMS)
  Shift+D    Read Distance + time to Top of Descent (once computed in flight,
             e.g. "145 miles to top of descent: 00:16:58")

Location:
  C          Read Nearest City

Trim:

  Ctrl+V     Activate/deactivate Visual Guidance mode. Does not require Hand

  Ctrl+T     Activate/deactivate Takeoff Assistance
  Ctrl+D     Read Distance to Selected Runway
  Ctrl+I     Read ILS Guidance System Report

Displays:
  Alt+P      Open the PFD window - the whole primary flight display, read a line
             at a time, with the bezel on the keyboard. See the G1000 section.
  Alt+M      Open the MFD window - pages, softkeys, the engine strip, checklists.
  Alt+N      Read Navigation Display
  Alt+E      Read CAS messages (the DA40's equivalent of an E/WD)

  Two keys that are named for an Airbus and do something else here, because this
  aeroplane has no lower ECAM and no ISIS but does have the things those keys are
  FOR:

  Alt+S      The ENGINE, at a glance. Load, RPM, oil pressure and temperature,
             coolant, gearbox, fuel flow, bus volts and amps - one press, the
             whole engine picture, each gauge with its arc. This is the single
             most useful key on a single-engine aeroplane: a sighted pilot takes
             the EIS strip in with one look.
  Alt+I      The STANDBY instruments. Airspeed, altitude, compass and the standby
             attitude gyro, plus a warning if that gyro is CAGED or has TOPPLED.
             The DA40 really carries all four, and a reversion to standbys is the
             one moment you need them together with no G1000 to read them from.

The G1000 windows (Alt+P and Alt+M):

  These are not read-outs - they are the DISPLAY, with its bezel on the keyboard.
  Arrow keys read the window; Enter presses the softkey on the selected row.

  Shift+Enter        The FMS cursor (CRSR). Nothing on a setup page can be
                     changed until this is on.
  Ctrl+Left/Right    Lower knob - moves BETWEEN fields with the cursor on, and
                     steps the page GROUP with it off.
  Ctrl+Up/Down       Upper knob - CHANGES the field you are on (opens its list of
                     choices, or cycles a letter in a text box), and steps the
                     PAGE with the cursor off.
  Ctrl+Enter         ENT - accepts the choice in an open list.
  Ctrl+G             List every page in the G1000 and open the one you pick. On
                     the PFD it lists that display's fifteen windows, five of
                     which have no bezel button at all.
  Ctrl+T             Type an ident straight into the waypoint box under the
                     cursor. The aircraft autocompletes it and reads back the
                     facility it found.
  Ctrl+D / Ctrl+F / Ctrl+P / Ctrl+E / Ctrl+L
                     Direct-To / Flight Plan / Procedures / Menu / Clear.
  Ctrl+PageUp/Down   Map range out / in.
  F5 / Escape        Refresh / close.

  Tuning the radios (PFD only - the G1000 tunes on the PFD and the MFD has no COM
  or NAV knob at all, for a sighted pilot either):

  Alt+Up/Down        COM megahertz         Alt+Left/Right      COM kilohertz
  Ctrl+Alt+Up/Down   NAV megahertz         Ctrl+Alt+Left/Right NAV kilohertz
  Alt+Enter          Move the COM tuning box between COM 1 and COM 2
  Ctrl+Alt+Enter     Move the NAV tuning box between NAV 1 and NAV 2

  The frequency speaks itself when the knob comes to rest. The Radios panel keeps
  its typed entry for when you already know the frequency.

Autopilot (GFC 700):
  The GFC 700 has NO absolute altitude set - the altitude preselect only moves in
  steps (1000 ft coarse, 100 ft fine), so altitude is entered by stepping rather
  than by typing a value. There is no autothrottle on either variant.

DA40-specific notes:

  Held controls. Several controls on this aeroplane are held rather than clicked,
  and MSFSBA holds them for you for the correct duration: the ECU test (about 26
  seconds, cycling the propeller once per ECU), the attitude-gyro cage, and the
  fuel-selector break wire. Press the button once and let it run.

  Silent switches. Panel controls make no cockpit sound. The aeroplane's switch
  sounds are attached to its mouse clickspots, so driving a control through
  MSFSBA bypasses them. Confirm a change by reading the value back, not by ear.

  Guards. The engine-master and emergency-battery guards are real controls you
  can open and close, but they do not physically block their switches in the
  simulator, and the model opens the engine-master guard by itself when that
  switch is operated.

  Cycling the propeller is an XLS item and does NOT exist on the NG. On the XLS
  the Lycoming drives a constant-speed propeller with its own blue pitch lever,
  and the run-up cycles it two or three times at about 1800 to 2000 RPM: it
  proves the governor responds, and it pushes the cold, thick oil out of the
  propeller hub so the blades will change pitch in flight. The NG has no
  propeller lever at all - the Austro's FADEC governs the propeller through the
  single power lever - so there is nothing to cycle. What the NG has in its place
  is the ECU test, which the Engine Start panel runs for you.

  Cold starts. On a cold engine the glow plugs run and the GLOW annunciation
  lights; the AFM has you wait for it to go out before cranking. The Engine Start
  panel shows glow state, the glow timer and plug temperature, so the wait is
  visible. A warm engine never shows it and starts immediately.

Automatic Announcements:
  Every switch and selector speaks when it changes outside MSFSBA - moved in the
  cockpit, by hardware, or by a failure. Numeric values (volts, temperatures,
  RPM, brightness) deliberately do not speak; they change constantly and would
  bury what matters. Read them from the Status Display instead.

  Use Ctrl+M to mute anything you would rather not hear.

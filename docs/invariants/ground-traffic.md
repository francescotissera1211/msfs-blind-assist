# Ground traffic and the runway watch — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/ground-traffic.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).
The text is verbatim from CLAUDE.md as of `1f37801a`; a trailing "→ doc" pointer is the original's. Cross-references such as "the bullet below", "above" or "under Core" point at CLAUDE.md's old single list, whose rules now live in several files: search `docs/invariants/` for the rule's key name to find it.

## TRF-1

- `GroundTrafficMonitor.SuppressCheck` must silence Caution/Warning callouts during the takeoff roll, when no taxi route is active, and during a landing rollout that is STILL ROLLING — but the forward-arc filter (120°) must never gate Awareness pings, and the Alt+G manual summary hotkey must stay ungated. On the landing exit at or above taxi speed (`RolloutExitGate.TaxiGroundSpeedKts`, 30 kt; unknown counts as fast) the monitor is NOT muted: it keeps evaluating and the runway watch keeps watching, and only what is SPOKEN is filtered (`GroundTrafficSuppression.LandingExitWarningsOnly` → the monitor's `LandingExitWarningsOnlyCheck`, `TrafficSpeechPolicy.SpeaksOnFastLandingExit`: Warning, RunwayCritical and RunwayInfo only) — KMEM 36L's two "Slow down" cautions at 44–47 kt wait, a genuine "Stop" still interrupts (at 40 kt an aircraft 250 ft ahead is under 4 s off), and a held-back line is not latched, so it speaks at taxi speed if it is still true. Never go back to muting the monitor there: that silenced the "Stop" and suspended the runway watch for the parallel an exit crosses. The rule lives in `Services/GroundTrafficSuppression` (pure, pinned), NOT as a lambda in MainForm, and the rolling qualifier (`> RolloutExitGate.NoExitStoppedGroundSpeedKts`) is load-bearing: the two stated reasons for the rollout arm — hands on the brakes, and the exit/runway-end callouts must not be talked over — both die when the aircraft stops, and since `RunwayEndCountdownGate` learned to sit through a mid-runway hold rather than calling it a backtrack, a pilot held on the runway by ATC stays in `LandingRollout` indefinitely and used to have every traffic callout dropped for the whole of it. An unknown ground speed counts as rolling. The runway watch has its own gate, `GroundTrafficSuppression.SuppressRunwayWatch` (see the runway-watch bullet), which outside takeoff assist is exactly `Suppress`'s rule. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-2

- **The departure-queue position counts the CONTIGUOUS line the pilot is in** (`GroundTrafficLogic.QueueAheadOf`, `QueueLinkMaxGapM` = 250 m) — the scan (`QueueScanM`, 3,000 m since 2026-11-26; at 1,500 m a line of 25 aircraft at 70 m ran past it and read "Number 22 in the queue" with the runway hold unseen) says how far to look, not where one queue ends — and **the wording is `GroundTrafficLogic.ReadQueue`'s alone**: "departure queue" ONLY on a route whose destination is the takeoff runway AND when the head of the pilot's OWN line (`QueueCluster.HeadAheadM`, never the farthest aircraft on the route) is within `QueueAtRunwayHoldM` (150 m) of the route end, BOTH measured from the aircraft. `GroundTrafficRouteContext.RouteEndMetres` is measured from the START of the current segment, so the monitor subtracts its own route position — compared unconverted, the real runway hold said "queue" and an intermediate one said "departure queue" (PR #247 review). A queued aircraft is on the route (≤ 30 m), ≤ 6 kt and POINTING ALONG it (±35°, by its EFFECTIVE direction — the track from a few seconds of position history when it is moving backwards, so a 2–4 kt pushback reads by its track even at the 1 s cadence) — pushbacks, crossing traffic, head-on creepers and parked aircraft never count — and on a queue route the intake keeps ground traffic out to `QueueScanM`, not only while a runway is watched. Under-counting a line with a wide gap in it is the safe failure against merging two lines. Pinned by `DepartureQueueClusterTests`, `QueueReadingTests` and `TrafficMotionModelTests`, and the route-end conversion end to end by `GroundTrafficMonitorHeadlessTests` (the pilot partway down a segment). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

- **A queued aircraft stays queued while it moves up, and a line that has stood at the runway hold stays the departure queue** (`QueueCandidate.WasQueued`, `TrackedGroundAircraft.QueueMember`, `ReadQueue`'s `inDepartureQueue`; 2026-11-26). Live at OMDB, five aircraft queued on M for runway 30R with the pilot behind them: as the line moved up one place at 8 kt the callouts read "Number 5, 4, 3, 2, 1" in ninety seconds with four aircraft still ahead, because an aircraft counted only while at 6 kt or less, and the same line was "the departure queue", "the queue" and "the departure queue" again, because its head is within 150 m of the route end only between one departure and the next aircraft reaching the hold. An aircraft never seen stopped on the route is still traffic taxiing ahead, never a queue (`An_aircraft_merely_taxiing_ahead_on_the_route_is_still_not_a_queue`). Once in the departure queue every queued aircraft ahead counts, whatever the 250 m link says: a pilot who sat while the line moved up 300 m was otherwise "Number 1". The head is "Number 1", as a controller says it, never "First", and the Alt+G summary names a queue only when there are aircraft ahead in it or the pilot is at the head of one that was announced: "First in the queue." ended every summary on a loaded route, with nobody ahead, and read as if the aircraft just listed were first. Pinned by `GroundTrafficLiveQueueTests` and `GroundTrafficQueueWordingTests`.

## TRF-3

- The ground-traffic **runway watch** never says a runway is "clear" — only "no traffic seen". Its scope is `RunwayWatchScopes.Resolve`: the held runway from `Navigation.HeldRunwayLabel` (the ONE derivation the status readout also uses — never a mirrored field: PR #247's `_heldRunwayLabel` was never assigned and no ordinary hold-short was ever watched), a Progressive hold (never an INTERMEDIATE holding point — `ProgressiveHoldRunwayResolver`), the backtrack, the lineup, the takeoff wait (takeoff assist's runway) and any runway under the aircraft — keyed on the RUNWAY (`RunwayKey`, both ends) and, when the watch has a REASON (takeoff wait, lineup, backtrack, hold, Progressive hold), on the runways that reason names ONLY (`RunwayWatch.IdentityKey`): another runway's pavement crossed or stood on widens the scan (`Runways`) but never changes the key, so hold → backtrack → lineup → takeoff wait is ONE watch that never restarts, even through an intersection; a position-only watch keeps the join of all its runways. The key the monitor compares is `RunwayWatch.Key`, which is that `IdentityKey` when a reason set one and that sorted join otherwise. A CROSSING is one watch too: a single-runway watch whose sources all end LINGERS (`RunwayWatchLinger`, same key, Holding mode; multi-runway watches never linger) until the aircraft is clear on the far side (half-width + 60 m), 30 m farther out on the side it started, or 60 s — never restore the stop-and-restart that spoke a second full status mid-crossing. A normal crossing lingers TWICE: at the hold-line gap (ended `reason=resumed` when the pavement names the runway again) and at the far edge (ended `reason=turned-away`, then `ev=watch stop`); `reason=clear` only when no tick saw the aircraft on the pavement. Its first status is ALWAYS spoken (never re-add a "lining up, stay silent" baseline, which recorded traffic on short final as already announced), waits up to 3 s for an aircraft over the pavement whose climb rate is not known yet (`LandingPending`, its first sample — never spoken), and follows the mode's interrupt rule (`RunwayEventsInterrupt`): on the runway (`OnRunway`, `LiningUp`, `TakeoffWait`) runway events interrupt — one withheld within `InterruptProtectMs` (3 s) of a "Stop" or another runway event is neither spoken nor latched, is re-evaluated every sweep and interrupts at the first evaluation after the window at which nothing more urgent, and nothing as urgent but nearer, is due, and is never moved to the queued channel (PR #247 focused re-review I1); at a hold (`Holding`) and while TURNING OFF after landing (`Vacating`: ONLY the runway landed on — `GroundTrafficRouteContext.LandingRunway`, the rollout's runway — under the aircraft on a landing-exit route, entered at ≥ `VacatingMinGsKts` (3 kt) and held down to `VacatingHoldGsKts` (1 kt) so decelerating through the turn never flips it; a parallel the exit route crosses is `OnRunway`) they are queued, so a runway status never cuts off taxi guidance's exit instructions; an unheld crossing or a stray runway entry is `OnRunway`. The SAME watch changing from a queuing mode (`Holding`, `Vacating`) into an interrupting one (`OnRunway`, `LiningUp`, `TakeoffWait`) — Continue at a hold, or stopping on the runway after landing — re-arms the first status ONCE per watch, CRITICAL-ONLY (`RunwayWatchScopes.ShouldRearmOnModeChange`; `ev=watch status-rearmed … reason=entered-runway|stopped-on-runway`) — from `Holding` only when the hold was RELEASED within `RunwayWatchScopes.RearmAfterHoldWindowMs` (10 s, inclusive; a judgement of how long a queued hold status can take to be spoken and heard, not a measurement) of that status being handed to the announcer, the window in which a PROMPT Continue can have cut it off (later, re-arming would only interrupt the Continue instruction with a status already heard; PR #247 re-review M4), from `Vacating` with no window: spoken, interrupting, only with something on the runway or on short final, otherwise completed silently and marking NOTHING known — because the queued status may have been cut off by the very `AnnounceImmediate` that moved the pilot, and since nothing was actually spoken, an occupant/final that showed up since the last spoken status must still reach the ordinary event path as a fresh one instead of being absorbed here with no callout (PR #247 B5 follow-up K2). The hold's RELEASE is the moment the crossing's linger BEGAN from the hold (`_holdReleasedUtc`, about the Continue press) — never the change to `OnRunway`, which comes only as the aircraft reaches the pavement, 11–15 s after Continue from a median hold line, and so almost never admitted a crossing, whose "Continuing." is exactly what cuts the hold status off — or, with no linger (a destination hold going straight to `LiningUp` or the backtrack), the change itself; a release time is dropped once a watch is adopted with no linger in progress, so a hold taken up again under the same key never lends it to a later change (PR #247 focused re-review N1). The watch ADOPTED THIS TICK — never the possibly-stale cycle a slow sweep completes with — decides BOTH whether a queuing evaluation waits for the re-armed status (PR #247 re-review M5; waiting on the stale cycle's own mode alone never ended once the pilot went back to a queuing mode before any interrupting evaluation completed, muting the whole runway watch for good — PR #247 re-review follow-up) and whether that status is critical (a stale `OnRunway` cycle completing once the pilot was moving again, `Vacating`, interrupted the exit instructions — PR #247 focused re-review N2); once that watch no longer interrupts, the re-armed status takes the silent K2 completion described above. It also decides the CHANNEL of every other runway line that can interrupt — the ordinary first status and every new occupant, new final or final turning short; "no traffic seen on the runway now" is always queued (PR #247 focused re-review S7: "on the runway, interrupt" and "never over the exit instructions" are facts about where the pilot is NOW; a sweep requested at a hold and answered once the pilot was on the runway queued a short final) — while the scan and the words stay the evaluated cycle's, so what is said can be up to one sweep old; the cycle's own mode is read only by the re-armed status's wait (it waits out a queuing cycle's sweep while the adopted watch interrupts). The re-arm is a mitigation for the FIRST status only: any runway line queued LATER — a new occupant or final queued at a hold or while vacating, "no traffic seen on the runway now" — can still be cut off by another interrupting line, and nothing re-arms it. A known occupant or final is forgotten only after `KnownAbsenceGraceMs` (3 s) unseen, so a one-sample classification drop never re-announces it — UNLESS the runway it is recorded under has itself left this evaluation's scan (a position-only widening that has since narrowed back down, above): that one is purged SILENTLY on the spot, never through the grace timer (PR #247 B5 follow-up K3). Known traffic is recorded by runway KEY (`WatchedRunway.Key`) — never the spoken designator, which for a position-only watch names the nearer end and flips at mid-runway, so the purge happens only when a runway leaves the scan, never on a name flip — in SEPARATE occupant and final records (`_knownOccupantRunway`, `_knownFinalRunway`), each cleaned only with its own kind; an id with no recorded key is left to the grace timer (`GroundTrafficLogic.IdsOutOfScope`), and the purge itself runs AFTER that evaluation's scan has refreshed every seen id's key, never before — an occupant standing inside an intersection is on both runways at once, and purging on the STALE key purged it and announced it again in the very evaluation that purged it (PR #247 re-review follow-up). Never share one record between them: a landing aircraft is a known occupant AND still a known final for the grace period after touchdown, and a shared map let the final's cleanup purge the occupant — every aircraft landing on the watched runway was announced twice (PR #247 re-review M1). "No traffic seen on the runway now" is PER RUNWAY (`GroundTrafficLogic.EmptiedRunwayKeys`): queued when the LAST known occupant recorded under that runway's key has been unseen for the grace period, never while an aircraft is seen on it, and never by the purge — purged traffic never feeds it; it names THAT runway, adding "Traffic still on final." only when that runway has a final, never every runway the watch scans (PR #247 re-review M2). It has its OWN gate, `GroundTrafficSuppression.SuppressRunwayWatch`: takeoff assist silences it only at a ground speed of `RunwayWatchTakeoffCutoffKts` (30) or more, or an UNKNOWN speed, which counts as rolling — the line-up wait is exactly when landing traffic matters — and a watch the gate closes on is SUSPENDED, not ended: the same key back within `RunwayWatchScopes.WatchResumeGraceMs` (15 s) resumes it with no new first status (`ev=watch suspend`/`ev=watch resume`) — a landing rollout's ~3 kt gate line flipped under a creeping pilot and restarted the watch each time. On resume the sweep-readiness reference is RE-ARMED to the resume moment, not left at the watch's original start (PR #247 integration follow-up R2): a sweep requested BEFORE the suspension, whose entries then arrived DURING it unwatched (dropping an aircraft on final) and whose completion only lands AFTER the resume, has its cycle's own gate check pass (it was open at request time) — only the readiness reference stops it, so leaving it at the original start reported a false "no traffic seen … or on final." A local route context's OWN runway list is always used, even an empty one; only with no local context do the airport's runways come from `RunwaySupplier` (a runway-only `TaxiGraph.Build`, cached per ICAO, cleared on a database switch). Airborne traffic is attributed to its best-fitting runway among ALL runways (`ClassifyAgainstRunways`; the 300 m + 12 % cone is wider than 1,228 fs2024 parallel-runway pairs), and an aircraft low over the pavement is `Landing` ONLY with a KNOWN climb ≤ `LandingMaxClimbFpm` (300 fpm) and no ground contact within `LandingGroundMemorySec` (60 s) — a departure just off the ground is never "landing". The first status waits for a sweep REQUESTED after the watch started — the monitor has its OWN rotating request ids (`REQUEST_GROUND_TRAFFIC` 600–607, `GroundTrafficSweepCompleted`; never TCAS's), drops a completion that is not the outstanding id, uses a small radius (`SweepRadiusMeters`) and never has more than one sweep outstanding. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-4

- The monitor's ground-traffic callouts go through `TrafficSpeechPolicy.Plan` (the Alt+G summary is not one of them: it speaks directly with `AnnounceImmediate` and stays ungated, TRF-1): ONLY `Warning` ("Stop"), `RunwayCritical` (runway events while ON the runway) and `Caution` ("Slow down") interrupt; only a STRICTLY more urgent interrupt may cut another off within `InterruptProtectMs` (3 s) — an equally urgent one no longer may either (PR #247 integration follow-up R3: faster polling made a second "Stop" routinely follow the first by about a second and cut it off) — and a withheld interrupt is neither spoken nor latched: it is re-evaluated every sweep and interrupts at the first evaluation after the window at which nothing more urgent, and nothing as urgent but nearer, is due, and it is NEVER moved to the queued channel (PR #247 focused re-review I1: R3 first gave it the alert slot, where it was latched on hand-over with no protect window of its own, so the next interrupt — even a less urgent one — cancelled a second "Stop", or an on-runway runway status naming a one-mile final, before it was heard, and it was never repeated); other alerts are queued, at most one per `AlertLineSpacingMs` (3 s); informational lines always go out; and every one-shot latch is committed in `TrafficCallout.OnEmitted` when the line is HANDED to the announcer (never while it is suppressed) — NOT when it is voiced: any later `AnnounceImmediate`, another feature's or the monitor's own safety line, can drop a queued line unheard, and the runway watch's critical-only re-arm is a mitigation for the watch's FIRST status only, not a cure — a runway line queued later can still be dropped unheard. "One interrupt per evaluation" was not enough: evaluations run about a second apart and every `AnnounceImmediate` cancels the lines queued behind it. A Caution/Warning re-escalation after a silent de-escalation needs a HIGHER zone than last spoken or the 15 s window (`EscalationRepeatWindowMs`) — except that a WARNING re-entry is also spoken once the aircraft is `EscalationReclosureFt` (50 ft) closer than when "Stop" was last spoken, and a withheld Warning escalation is never recorded silently (`ZoneToRecordWhenWithheld`), so creeping back toward traffic after a "Stop" is warned again while boundary flicker at a constant distance is not. Caution keeps R8: complying with "Slow down" never earns another one inside the window, and a Caution that keeps closing reaches the always-spoken "Stop" at the Warning line. The old CLAUDE.md claim that the window "may suppress Awareness only" is retired. Moving-away is a RATE (`IsMovingAway`, 20 ft per 3 s) OR, for traffic moving at 3 kt or more, MOTION (`IsOpeningByMotion`: the relative velocity opening the gap at 1 m/s or more, or its lead ALONG the route growing that fast — the rate needs a previous evaluation, so traffic pulling away was "Stop" on the first one); a Warning withheld because the traffic moves away is never recorded (`ZoneToRecordWhenWithheld`, as for one the repeat window withholds), so "Stop" speaks once the traffic stops OPENING inside the Warning distance — recording it swallowed that "Stop" for good, already on the reviewed base (05e5d3ea), not only after the motion port. A "Stop" withheld BECAUSE the traffic was opening is then HELD (`IsMovingAwayOrHeld`, `ev=stop-hold`) while that traffic moves at 3 kt or more and the pilot is not closing on it at 0.5 m/s or more (closing on BOTH the straight-line gap and the 2 s along-route lead, `LeadRateMps` — never the one-second lead alone: in a turn the pilot's own route position jitters and round a bend the straight-line gap closes while the leader pulls away, so one such sample said "Stop, … very close" about an aircraft pulling away; VirtualPilot traffic A/B: 15 false calls fixed over 3,478 taxis, 0 new), and speaks once it stops, the pilot closes, or — whatever the speeds say — the gap reaches `StopHoldFloorFt` (200 ft, PR #247 integration follow-up R1: without it a pilot closing more slowly than the release speed on a leader still moving at 3 kt or more was never warned, however close it got; the floor is scoped to the HELD half only, never to traffic genuinely opening right now) — following a departing leader out of a queue drew "Stop" with the gap still growing; never "record it until the traffic stops" (a leader slowing to 4 kt would never re-arm). A Caution/Warning withheld OUTSIDE the forward arc is recorded by the same rule, so turning to face very close traffic that was behind still earns "Stop". Converging needs the forward arc or a moving pilot (`ConvergingAllowed`). With a route, "Slow down"/"Stop" need a real threat (`GroundTrafficLogic.IsRouteThreat`): near the route ahead, very close, or MOVING traffic (3 kt) on a predicted collision course — never a PARKED aircraft's straight-line closest approach, which assumes the pilot keeps going straight through a bend (the PR author measured 421 false calls over 100 airports). That trade is deliberate and has a cost: a parked aircraft OFF the route never earns "Slow down", only "Stop" inside the fixed 250 ft — so a pilot rolling at 3 kt or more with ANY ground aircraft within ±30° of the nose inside the speed-scaled Caution distance keeps the sweep at 1 s (`NeedsFastPoll`), which brings that "Stop" within a second of the line (at 12 kt the 3 s cadence gave it at about 200 ft). Never buy the "Slow down" back by restoring the parked closest-approach threat. Only the FIRST aircraft on the route ahead gets on-route and zone callouts, and only traffic OCCUPYING the route — stopped on it or moving along it (`OccupiesRoute`) — can be that first (a crossing aircraft hid the stopped one beyond it): one more than 10 m further along is queued behind it (`IsQueuedBehindFirst`) and gets neither — except "Stop", which is never withheld (`WithholdsZoneBehindFirst`) — and head-on traffic is never queued behind it. A queued aircraft's zone is recorded, so one that becomes the first is not called at the zone it had and, from Caution, loses its "on your route" too. The monitor has a headless harness (`IGroundTrafficSimSource`, `TickForHarness`, a simulated clock; `GroundTrafficMonitorHeadlessTests`, `GroundTrafficMonitorRuleTests`): every rule reads the injected clock (`_utcNow`), never `DateTime.UtcNow` — on a real clock a test's ticks all fall inside one 3 s alert spacing and hide defects — and a fake sweep is answered only after its request returns, since the monitor learns the id it waits for from that call. A rule with two protections needs a test per protection wherever one can tell them apart: the runway watch's first status has one for the readiness gate (a watch restarted under the same key) and one for R2's re-arm of it on resume (PR #247 integration follow-up R2: a sweep requested BEFORE the suspension, whose completion lands after the resume, which its own cycle lets through) — a sweep requested before any watch existed is stopped by both, so it pins neither — and a RE-ARMED first status has one for its wait for a sweep requested in the interrupting mode (the M5 wait, PR #247 focused re-review S8), one for that wait's bound (it waits only while the adopted watch interrupts — replay G) and one for its critical decision (the adopted watch's, never its sweep's mode — X3/N2) (PR #247 focused re-review S9). Of the completion's own cycle, the watch-gate check has no test that can tell it apart: since R2 a sweep requested WHILE the watch was suspended is stopped by the re-armed readiness gate as well, so for the first status that check is defence in depth (PR #247 focused re-review M1); the watch its request saw is pinned by S7's first test (its wording) and by S8's (its mode, the M5 wait). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-5

- "… ahead is moving." comes from a LATCHED departure (`QueueMovementPolicy.Step`) and is spoken only for the NEAREST aircraft directly ahead — never a one-sample speed edge, which was lost whenever the call was outranked or another sweep moved the previous speed on. "Move up" (`QueueMovementPolicy.EvaluateNudge`) is an instruction ATC has not given, so it speaks only where `GroundTrafficRouteContext.AllowsQueuePrompt` (Taxiing on a joined route) and the pilot is not on runway pavement, only to a pilot below 1 kt, never with anything ELSE within 250 ft ahead — the aircraft whose departure armed it does not count TOWARD THAT DISARM RULE ONLY, and only while it is still MOVING (above `GroundTrafficLogic.QueueStoppedGs`, 1.5 kt — `QueueMovementPolicy.NearestOtherAheadFt`; it arms a few feet from where it sat, so counting it disarmed the nudge a second after arming; once it stops again it counts like any other aircraft, so a leader that crept 20–40 ft and stopped inside 250 ft disarms the nudge instead of drawing a "Move up. 200 feet to the traffic ahead." into a gap there is no room for — PR #247 re-review M3) — at most 3 times 20 s apart; it arms only when the aircraft moved off ALONG the route and disarms at 2 kt. The SPOKEN distance names whichever aircraft is nearest ahead, that one INCLUDED — it is genuinely the traffic the pilot will close on — never the disarm-only distance, which once made a leader still just ahead get reported as "The traffic ahead has taxied on." (PR #247 B5 follow-up K1). While the departed leader is still moving but still within 250 ft, the nudge neither disarms nor speaks: it waits, armed, and speaks once the gap has opened (`EvaluateNudge`; a leader creeping on at 1.6–2.5 kt drew "Move up. 210 feet to the traffic ahead.", inside the Warning distance — PR #247 focused re-review N3). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-9

## TRF-10

- The SAME watch changing from a queuing mode (`Holding`, `Vacating`) into an interrupting one (`OnRunway`, `LiningUp`, `TakeoffWait`) re-arms the first status ONCE per watch, CRITICAL-ONLY (`RunwayWatchScopes.ShouldRearmOnModeChange`; `ev=watch status-rearmed`). From `Holding` it re-arms only when the hold was RELEASED within `RunwayWatchScopes.RearmAfterHoldWindowMs` (10 s, inclusive) of that status being handed to the announcer. The release is the moment the crossing's linger BEGAN from the hold (`_holdReleasedUtc`, about the Continue press), never the change to `OnRunway`, which comes 11–15 s after Continue from a median hold line and so almost never admitted a crossing; with no linger it is the change itself. A release time is dropped once a watch is adopted with no linger in progress, so a hold taken up again under the same key never lends it to a later change. A re-armed status with nothing on the runway or on short final completes silently and marks NOTHING known: the queued status may have been cut off unheard (PR #247 B5 follow-up K2). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-12

- Every aircraft distance the ground-traffic monitor SPEAKS (the zone callouts, "on your route … ahead", "Move up", the runway occupants, the Alt+G summary) and the Awareness, Caution and Warning zones themselves are the GAP between the two aircraft's outlines: `GroundTrafficLogic.HullGapM` over each one's `AircraftHull`, nose to tail, tail to nose or wingtip to wingtip, whichever parts are nearest. Never a centre distance again.
- A hull is, most exact first: the installed model's own silhouette (`GroundTrafficModelCatalog` by the title the sweep reports, measured by `GroundTrafficModelReader`); the published length and span of the type (`GroundTrafficAircraftSizes`, read from the title, then the ATC type and model); the wing span the simulator reports, scaled; `AircraftHull.Default`, the 61 m widebody. An unknown aircraft is never guessed SMALLER than that: a gap spoken too small is the safe side.
- The real-time rules read the gap too, each at the figure its old centre distance meant for the reference widebodies (200 ft less): the queue-moving cone (`IsInQueueCone`, `QueueAheadRangeFt` 400), "Move up" (`QueueMovementPolicy.NudgeMinGapFt` 50), the held-"Stop" floor (`StopHoldFloorFt` 15) and the 1 s sweep cadence (`GroundGeometryForPoll`, `NeedsFastPoll`). The closest-approach test for converging traffic places both outlines where they will be at the closest approach and reads the gap there (`CONFLICT_DCPA_M` 15 m to warn, 60 m to re-arm, `ThreatDcpaM` 30 m), and the Alt+G summary's nearest aircraft are the nearest by gap. What still reads the two reported POINTS: the tracking and sweep ranges and the moving-away rates (a rate is the same either way).

Why. Until 2026-11 the zones were centre distances of 600, 400 and 250 ft, "the wanted gap plus about 200 ft for two 787s' half-fuselages", and the number spoken was the centre distance. That is right for two widebodies in line and wrong for everything else: `ground_traffic.log` of 2026-10-08 holds "Stop, BE58 very close, ahead and to the right, 75 metres." for a Baron 9 m long, and a pilot pushing back toward a heavy heard about 80 metres with a few metres of clear space. The simulator cannot supply the size: it has no length variable, and its `WING SPAN` is whatever the model's flight model says (61.87 m for every AIG 777, 15.48 m for a SayIntentions 777 and for most IVAO models; measured at OMDB on 2026-11-26). AIG's ATC model is only a family ("$$:Triple 7", "$$:737" for a MAX 8 and a BBJ alike), so the type is read from the model title first.

What the measurement rests on (`GroundTrafficModelReader`, checked with `tools/TrafficModelAudit` against every model installed on 2026-11-26: 1,022 model files, 23,986 titles; AIG 428 models within 3% of the published length and 5% of the span and 64 variants, FSLTL 98 and 20, IVAO 254 and 94, the COWS DA40s, the iniBuilds A350-900 66.95 x 64.78 m and -1000 73.93 m, the PMDG 777-300ER 73.03 x 64.71 m and 777F 63.52 m, the Fenix A320 37.72 m long, 34.22 m wide with fences and 36.04 m with sharklets; 2 unreadable, IVAO's follow-me car and its invisible placeholder):

- The model's origin is the point `PLANE LATITUDE/LONGITUDE` reports, +Z is the nose. `reference_datum_position` moves the flight model's datum, never that point. Checked in the simulator on 2026-11-26 without a camera: an aircraft spawned on the ground has its reported point exactly `static_cg_height` above it whatever its datum, and for the two models whose datum is a metre off the origin VERTICALLY in opposite directions the model's own geometry says which point that is: IVAO's C-130 (datum 1.0 m below the origin, reported point 2.590 m up, tail top 9.07 m above the origin, real height 11.66 m) and AIG's Citation Excel (datum 0.91 m above, reported 1.158 m, tail top 4.11 m, real 5.24 m) both stand on the ground to within 3 cm with the reported point at the ORIGIN and a metre wrong with it at the datum. FSLTL's 747-400F has its datum 25 m ahead of the origin, so the reading matters.
- MSFS packs a mesh's materials into shared accessors and says in `extras.ASOBO_primitive` which slice is each primitive's (`PrimitiveCount` triangles from `StartIndex`). The MSFS 2024 exporter (iniBuilds, PMDG, Fenix) counts a slice's indices from its `BaseVertexIndex`; read without it a later slice is drawn with the first slice's vertices. 829 of the 6,218 aircraft glTF files installed pack this way; the traffic libraries write `StartIndex` alone, with indices from vertex 0.
- What is drawn with the simulator's INVISIBLE material (`ASOBO_material_invisible`, unless `"enabled": false`) or as an environment occluder is never seen and never airframe: the 31 m sphere the PMDG 777 hangs on each lamp, the 35 m boxes on the A350's wings, the Fenix's 200 m triangles and fuselage box, collision shapes, FSLTL's gear helpers. A NAME is no test for those. A rule that dropped any node called a light threw away the whole airframe of 353 AIG models (an FSX-converted model keeps it under a visibility condition called `cabin_lights_VC`; a Learjet 45 measured 8.7 m) and the wingtips of AIG's 787: the audit showed AIG fall from 428 models within tolerance to 223. `NotAirframe` now names only ground equipment drawn with ordinary materials (IVAO's tug, fuel truck, stairs, chocks).
- A skinned mesh (a flexing wing) is taken in model space, its node's transform not applied, and among the three most detailed levels one with no skinned mesh is preferred: applying the node moved the A330's and A350's wings 17 m sideways. The coarser levels drop parts (the PMDG 777's sixth has no tail cone), and a level over 48 MB is passed over for a lighter one.
- A node an animation moves but that declares no transform of its own takes the animation's first key (the Dash 8-400's flaps otherwise lie 21 m along the wing). A node a gear, wheel or steering animation moves is left out with everything under it (FSLTL's 777 nose gear rests 6 m ahead of the nose; AIG's gear rests half retracted).
- Each station takes the narrower of its two sides and only the one connected body is kept (a part more than 2 m clear of it is dropped); a mesh of at most 36 vertices spread more than 40 m both ways is a helper plane whatever it is drawn with.
- An MSFS 2024 MODULAR aircraft is assembled as the simulator assembles it (`GroundTrafficModelCatalog.Parts`): the common model, then every `[SIM_ATTACHMENT.N]` and `[MERGE_MODEL.N]` that goes to the exterior and the attachments of those in turn, each placed at its `attach_to_node`; a model XML's `<LODS Template>` and `<MergeModel>`; a file another package holds (`Vfs`: the Fenix's parts are in its common package, a DA40 livery names the base aircraft's model folder); and a glTF scene tagged with a node's `ASOBO_unique_id`, which hangs on that node (the Fenix's fences and sharklets on the wing's tip nodes: without it every Fenix was 32.58 m wide). Stand-in A319 and A321 presets built from the parts the Fenix's common package ships measure 33.99 and 44.68 m long.
- An FSX-format `.MDL` (IVAO's 737-200, -400 and -500) is read from its `VERT` and `INDE` chunks through the `PART` records of its most detailed `LODE` (the largest level; the first listed is three vertices).

A measured outline more than 15% from its type's published length or span is not believed (`GroundTrafficAircraftSizes.Agrees`): the type's size is used, as it is for a title whose model lives in a streamed or encrypted package (the Asobo aircraft IVAO borrows, MSFS 2024's own passive traffic) and until a model seen for the first time has been measured in the background. The own aircraft's outline is the one measured, never widened to the wing span its flight model reports: the PMDG 777's says 62.7 m for a wing that is, and measures, 64.7 m.

Nothing is a maintained list of models: the index is the installed packages' own `aircraft.cfg` files, read once per session (about 11 s in the background) and again (at most every `ReindexMinutes`) only when a title is not found AND a package folder or its `SimObjects\Airplanes` folder has changed (`PackagesStamp`), and an outline is kept under its model file's path, size and date (`%APPDATA%\MSFSBlindAssist\traffic-models\outlines.json`; bump `GroundTrafficModelCatalog.CacheVersion` when the measurement changes). The size table and the title patterns are only the fallback; `tools/TrafficModelAudit` shows every model and title they miss.

The distances TRF-1 to TRF-11 quote in their histories (250 ft, 200 ft, 600 ft) are the centre figures of their day; the rule lines above them carry today's.

Added 2026-11-26.

## TRF-13

- The own aircraft's direction of travel is `GroundTrafficMonitor.OwnDirection`: its nose heading unless its track (`_ownHistory`, the previous position pushed by `StoreOwnPosition`) shows it moving tail-first, when it is the track, the rule `GroundTrafficLogic.EffectiveDirection` reads a traffic aircraft's pushback by. The closing speeds, the closest approach, the `ForwardArcDeg` arc "Slow down" and "Stop" are given for (`TrafficView.RelTravel`), "converging" and the fast poll all follow it, and while it is pushed back there is no route to judge a threat by (the route lies ahead of the nose). What is SPOKEN stays relative to the nose: the aircraft is "behind".
- STOPPED, the aircraft keeps the direction it last moved in (`_ownReversed`): a pushback that has stopped is still a pushback, and what is behind the tail is still in its way. Read as nose-first the moment it stopped, the 777 it had just been told to stop for was "Clear" six seconds later, still 35 m behind the tail (OMDB, 2026-11-26, live). When the direction of travel changes (the tug is gone and the aircraft taxies out) every unanswered "Stop" is dropped silently: the aircraft left behind is never announced clear. Pushed back, nothing is read or said about a queue.
- The own track needs `OwnReversingMinMoveM` (1 m), not the 3 m a traffic aircraft's needs: the own position is the simulator's own and exact, a tug's 3 kt is 1.5 m a second, and with 3 m the first two seconds of a pushback away from an aircraft off the nose still drew "Stop" for it.

Why. The simulator gives the monitor a heading and an UNSIGNED ground speed. Live at OMDB on 2026-11-26 an A350 was pushed back 53 m toward a 777 stopped across the taxilane behind its stand, from 59 m of clear space to 25, and nothing was said: read nose-first, what it backed into lay outside the arc and was "moving away". With the fix the same pushback at 3 kt gave "Slow down, 777 behind, 70 metres" and "Stop, 777 very close, behind, 25 metres", and the tug stopped with 24.5 m between the tail and the 777's wingtip, measured with the app's own code from the simulator's positions. Pinned by `GroundTrafficPushbackTests`.

Not covered, by design until its own change: with NO taxi guidance running the monitor is silent ([TRF-1]), pushback included, and a pilot waiting at the stand for "when clear of the A320 behind, pushback approved" has only Alt+G.

Added 2026-11-26.

## TRF-14

- Every "Stop" is answered once. `TrackedGroundAircraft.StopSpokenUtc` is set when "Stop" is spoken for an aircraft and cleared by the answer: "Clear of Delta A320." (`TrafficCalloutKind.Clear`, an alert-slot line, never an interrupt) once `GroundTrafficLogic.IsClearOf` holds, at least `ClearMinSecondsAfterStop` (3 s) after the "Stop"; or "... ahead is moving" for the aircraft dead ahead within the queue watch's reach (`IsInQueueCone`), where "Clear of" is never said as well.
- Clear means: no longer in the arc the pilot travels into ([TRF-13]), or MOVING with the gap opening at 1 m/s or more and beyond the "Slow down" distance. Never for a stopped aircraft still in that arc, however far back the pilot stopped: at 30 kt "Stop" comes with 120 m to go, and a pilot who brakes at once stops outside the "Slow down" distance behind an aircraft that is still there.

Why. A pilot told "Stop" for crossing traffic (a "give way" instruction), or pushed back toward an aircraft that then taxied on, heard nothing more and had to ask with Alt+G whether it had gone; only the aircraft ahead in a line was ever answered. Live at OMDB on 2026-11-26, own aircraft at 15 kt and an A320 crossing right to left at 20 kt: "converging from the right, about 25 seconds", "Slow down ... 110 metres", "Stop ... 65 metres", and 14 s later "Clear of ... A320." Pinned by `GroundTrafficClearTests`.

The callouts' timing at every taxi speed is pinned by `GroundTrafficSpeedMatrixTests` (own and traffic at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 kt): "Stop" for an aircraft in the way comes its lead before a 100 ft gap (48 m at 5 kt, 66 at 10, 84 at 15, 115 at 20, 152 at 25, 195 at 30) and always leaves five seconds and more before the two would touch, also when catching up slower traffic; "converging, about N seconds" is within six seconds of the truth at all 36 pairs.

- "Stop" for an aircraft IN THE WAY is given for 100 ft of gap (`STOP_BEHIND_GAP_FT`, 30 m, plus the speed lead), not the 50 ft of `WARNING_GAP_FT`: on or near the route ahead (never while pushed back, when the route is not being followed), or within `QueueAheadConeDeg` of the direction of travel. Owner's decision of 2026-11-26, after asking what "very close, 60 metres" meant: there is no regulatory taxi following distance, practice is about an aircraft length, and 50 ft left a widebody's nose in the idle exhaust of the one ahead. An aircraft BESIDE the path keeps 50 ft: taxilane wingtip clearances are under 30 m by design, and every aircraft parked on a stand the pilot passes would otherwise be a "Stop". The "genuinely close" test of `IsRouteThreat` stays 50 ft for the same reason.
- The lead added to every zone boundary is `GroundTrafficLogic.ZoneLeadFt`: seven seconds of travel, or the distance a `StopReactionSec` (3 s) reaction and braking at `GentleBrakingMps2` (1 m/s2) take, whichever is more. The flat seven seconds are generous at 10 kt (36 m against 29) and short from about 16 kt up, braking distance growing with the square of the speed: a pilot who took two seconds and braked gently stopped 10 m short of the aircraft ahead from 25 kt and hit it from 30. The owner noticed it watching a scripted pilot that stopped dead on "Stop" (2026-11-26). The braking term is capped at `GentleBrakingMaxKts` (30 kt): on a landing exit at 45 kt nobody brakes gently, and it would put "Stop" 370 m out. Pinned by `A_pilot_who_takes_two_seconds_and_brakes_gently_stops_short` (39 to 48 m of clear space left at every speed from 5 to 30 kt).

Added 2026-11-26.

## TRF-15

- The lead in the Awareness, Caution and Warning zones (`ZoneLeadFt`, [TRF-14]) is worked out from the PILOT's speed. Traffic that is itself coming at the pilot takes room off the gap as well, whatever the pilot does, so `EvaluateProximity` adds `ZONE_LEAD_SEC` (7 s) of the traffic's own closing speed, `TrafficView.ItsClosingMps` (its velocity along the line between the two, never negative), to all three zones for that aircraft. Measured before (`GroundTrafficSpeedMatrixTests.Head_on_traffic_Stop_leaves_the_time_a_stopped_aircraft_does`): two A320s nose to nose at 10 kt each had "Stop" 3.5 s from touching, where a stopped aircraft ahead leaves 7; now every pair from 5 to 30 kt each has 7.5 to 12 s.
- Only for traffic that will come near: moving (`MovingTrafficKts`), time to the closest approach still ahead, and the gap at that closest approach within `ThreatDcpaM` (30 m). An aircraft going the other way on the parallel taxiway closes as fast as head-on traffic and never comes near: it gets no lead, no "Slow down" and no "Stop" (`Traffic_going_the_other_way_on_the_parallel_taxiway_is_never_Stop`, all 36 speed pairs).
- Live, OMDB taxiway M, 2026-11-26: an A320 taxiing toward the pilot at 10 kt, the pilot at 10 kt: "Slow down ... ahead, 110 metres", "Stop ... very close, ahead, 85 metres" three seconds later, about 8 s from touching; the pilot stopped in 24 m (two seconds, then 1 m/s2), and "Clear of" came once the A320 had turned off the taxiway.

## TRF-16

- What a traffic aircraft is CALLED is decided in two functions and nowhere else: `GroundTrafficLogic.NamingType` (the type) and `GroundTrafficLogic.SpokenAirline` (the airline), both fed by `OnAiTrafficReceived`. Measured over the 30,747 titles installed on the owner's PC on 2026-11-26 (AIG 10,655, IVAO MTL 9,430, FSLTL 3,878, the rest add-ons): every type resolved, but only some 50 designators had a spoken name, so everything that was not a mainline airliner was read out as its code ("FLEXJET E55P", "GLEX", "C56X", "B734", "DHC6"), a type outside the size table was named by its maker ("Panavia", "Sikorsky": what the ATC type holds), and no IVAO model had an airline.
- The type, best first: the one the title or the ATC strings name (`GroundTrafficAircraftSizes.ResolveIcao`); the designator the aircraft's own package declares (`icao_type_designator` in its aircraft.cfg, `GroundTrafficModelCatalog.Declared`) or the type the simulator reports, whichever has a spoken name; a title that is itself a designator (IVAO's "A5"); then the declared designator as it is written. The title stays first because packages mis-declare (IVAO declares "MD8X" for every MD-80, ApronLife "B772" for a jetway helper).
- `SpokenTypes` (`GroundTrafficLogic.TypeNames.cs`) holds the name of every designator those libraries resolve to (335) and every one the size table knows (`GroundTrafficNamingTests` pins both lists), said the way it is said on the radio: Airbus keep their number, Boeing lose the B, the rest get their name ("Phenom 300", "King Air 200", "Twin Otter", "C-130"). An entry of "" is a placeholder that names no type (ZZZZ). A designator missing from the table is read as its code, as all were before: never dropped, never replaced by the maker.
- The airline: the one the model gives (ATC AIRLINE; in AIG and FSLTL the radio name, "SPEEDBIRD"); else the name the INSTALLED liveries give the airline code the package declares for that livery (`icao_airline`), or the three-letter code the callsign begins with ("RYR123"). `GroundTrafficModelCatalog.AirlineNames` learns code to name from every livery that declares both (1,553 codes on that PC): the name most of that code's liveries carry, in the spelling that is not all capitals when there is one. Only three-letter codes (AIG marks private aircraft with made-up longer ones, "GA2J"), and a callsign counts only as three letters then a digit, so a registration never becomes an airline. 6,485 of the 9,430 IVAO titles get an airline this way; the rest keep their callsign.
- The catalog knows what the packages declare only once it has read them, on its worker thread, when the first traffic title is asked for. `IndexVersion` counts those reads, and `TrackedGroundAircraft.NameDeclaredVersion` has each name rebuilt when it moves, so an aircraft first named from the simulator's strings alone ("RYR 123, Panavia") is renamed a second or two later with nothing new from the simulator.
- `tools/TrafficModelAudit` ends with the installed types that have no spoken name and the number of airline codes learnt: run it after a traffic library is added or updated, and add the names it lists.
- Live, OMDB, 2026-11-26, ten aircraft spawned round the pilot: "FLEXJET Phenom 300", "N152WP, Cessna 152", "Mandarin ATR 72", "Netherlands Air Force C-130", "German Air Force Tornado" (an IVAO model with callsign GAF123), "DHADA, EC135", "Speedbird A320" (an IVAO model with callsign BAW45K), "Caribbean Airlines 737", "NORTH-SHORE Global Express", "BROADSWORD King Air 200"; and on the runway watch "Caribbean Airlines 737 on runway 30R, stopped, ahead and to the right, 230 metres."
